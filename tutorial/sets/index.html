A whole assignment¶

An assignment is more than questions drawn one at a time. It has parts, a range of difficulty, no two questions alike. A set says all of that in the file, and rhema turns it into one paper.

# Week 3: sequences, differentiation and logic, as one assignment.

import "questions.rhm" as seq
import "calculus.rhm" as calc
import "boolean.rhm" as bool

set week3 {
  part "Sequences"
    1 of seq.main
  part "Differentiation"
    3 of calc.derivative(cost ~ 6 spread 2 low 2)
    1 of calc.integral(cost in {5, 7})
  part "Logic"
    3 of bool.main(cost in {8, 10, 12})
  distinct
  balanced
  ordered
}

The file has no question of its own. It imports the sequence question of chapter 5 and two library grammars, and declares a set:

$ rhema run assignment.rhm --run week3

Entries¶

3 of calc.derivative(cost ~ 6 spread 2 low 2)

An entry asks for a number of questions of one type: main or a named question of this file, or alias.name from an imported module. In brackets, it says how to start the question's budgets, in one of three ways:

cost = 6
Every question of the entry starts at 6.
cost in {8, 10, 12}
Each question takes one of the values, and the list is used up before any value repeats: three questions get 8, 10 and 12 in some order.
cost ~ 6 spread 2
Budgets are drawn around 6, with a spread of about 2, and then adjusted so that their mean is exactly 6. low 2 keeps every budget at 2 or above. allowed {4, 6, 8} restricts the draw to those values, for a grammar whose forms only exist at certain prices; the mean is then as close to 6 as they allow.

The last form is how difficulty is meant to be set for an assignment: not "every derivative costs 6", which makes them all alike, but "the derivatives average 6", which gives some easier and some harder, at a known level overall. The listing ends with the mean of each part's budgets to show it.

N of any(A, B) draws each of the N questions from the listed types evenly.

Parts and numbering¶

part "Heading" starts a part. Its questions are numbered 1(a), 1(b) and so on, under the heading. A set without part lines numbers each question on its own: 1, 2, 3.

Constraints¶

distinct
No two questions have the same shape: the same productions, in the same places, with only the numbers different. 3 + 4 and 7 + 2 are one shape. Questions of different types are never repeats of each other, with one exception in the 0.1.1 release: two named questions on the same rule, such as seq.nth and seq.sum, count as repeats there. The repository has the fix.
balanced
No three questions in a row with the same coded answer, for questions answered with true/false or a small code such as 0, 1, 2.
ordered
Within a part, easier questions first, by total budget.

When a constraint cannot be met, such as distinct over more questions than a grammar has shapes, rhema says which question it could not place instead of trying for ever.

The same paper every time¶

The set's name is the assignment's name, and the paper is fixed by it: running week3 today and next month gives the same questions. Chapter 8 covers giving each student a paper of their own, and getting the paper into WeBWorK.
